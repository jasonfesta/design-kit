Portrait direction and clinical daylight

Ground preventive health pages in warm architectural daylight and candid human dialogue rather than sterile hospital corridors. Preserve stable four-to-three slots during network loading and whenever clinician privacy policies omit portraits.

When to use

  • Use oak-table consultation photographs beside medical explainers so preventive visits look welcoming and unhurried.
  • Reserve right-aligned morning portraits gazing leftward for hero stages carrying editorial copy on the left.
  • Render porcelain shimmer frames during decode and structured clinician credentials when no photo is shown.

Composition and copy rules

  • Always compose portraits so people gaze inward toward headlines and actions, never outward past the browser edge.
  • Leave skin tones and oak daylight untouched in dark mode, framing images with a faint inner ring.
  • Pair each room photograph with a specific caption naming the visit step and consultation length.

Plain-English biomarker range cards

Translate coded lab printouts into horizontal target bands and everyday physician guidance without clinical line graphs. Show optimal, borderline, and above-range biomarker states side by side.

When to use

  • Use range cards on marketing explainers to show how Wardline reports lipid, insulin, and inflammation readings.
  • Replace intimidating multi-line laboratory charts with single-marker horizontal target bars.

Composition and copy rules

  • Mark each reading with a vertical rectangular caliper over a shaded target zone rather than circular warning dots.
  • Pair every number with a plain-English explanation of what to maintain or review next.
  • Bind readings and clinical units with non-breaking spaces in tabular lining numerals.
