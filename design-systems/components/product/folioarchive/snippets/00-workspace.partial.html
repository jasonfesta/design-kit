Colour as Structure, 1954–1978

32 postwar paintings and works on linen tracing how unmixed pigment, edge tension, and ruled graphite intervals came to define architectural space on their own.

Galleries 3 to 5, Chipperfield Wing · Kunsthaus Zürich · Through 14 February

Curatorial essay

When Helene Vonderlan hung her first masonite panels in Zürich in the autumn of 1958, visitors noticed the complete absence of frames. Working straight from unmixed tubes of cadmium yellow, Venetian red, and raw ochre, she demonstrated that two neighbouring hues alter each other's apparent warmth long before the eye registers a drawn contour.

In Paris, Mateo Salvat cut matte mineral ultramarine diagonally across unprimed chalk ground so the plaster gallery wall became half of the composition, while Clara Vermeer ruled fine pencil horizons across pale gessoed linen until pigment gave way almost entirely to north skylight.
